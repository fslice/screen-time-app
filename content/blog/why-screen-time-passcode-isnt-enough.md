## The Problem With Willpower

Every January, millions of people set the same goal: less screen time. They open Settings, tap Screen Time, and set a passcode. Maybe they even ask a friend to type it in so they don't know it.

It lasts about a week.

The issue isn't that you're weak. It's that **the system is designed to be easy to bypass**. Apple built Screen Time as a parental control tool, not an addiction intervention. The "Ignore Limit" button is one tap away. The passcode recovery is tied to your own Apple ID. And if all else fails, a factory reset wipes everything clean.

You're fighting a system that wants you to keep scrolling.

## What Most People Try

Most approaches to screen time fall into three categories:

- **Pure willpower** — just deciding to use your phone less. Works for approximately nobody long-term.
- **App blockers** — third-party apps that sit on top of your OS. Most can be uninstalled in seconds.
- **Accountability partners** — asking someone else to set your passcode. Works until they're asleep, busy, or you guilt them into telling you.

The problem with all three is the same: **the path back to your apps is too short**. When you're tired, bored, or stressed, the friction between you and Instagram needs to be measured in minutes, not seconds.

This is the core insight: **you don't need more willpower. You need more friction.**

### How Friction Changes Behavior

Research in behavioral economics has shown that even small increases in friction dramatically reduce action. Making organ donation opt-out instead of opt-in increased participation by 60%. Adding one extra click to a checkout flow drops conversion rates by 7%.

The same principle works in reverse. If checking Twitter requires 15 minutes of typing random words, you'll check it a lot less. Not because you can't — you always *can* — but because the effort isn't worth it for a quick dopamine hit.

### Why 600 Words Specifically

We tested different thresholds:

- **100 words** — too fast. About 2 minutes. Not enough friction to break an impulse.
- **200 words** — moderate. Some people find this sufficient for casual habits.
- **400 words** — the sweet spot for most users. About 10 minutes of focused typing.
- **600 words** — maximum friction. By the time you're done, you've had time to reflect, reconsider, and usually decide it wasn't worth it.

The default is 600 because the people who seek out a tool like Latch are usually past the point of gentle nudges.

## The Factory Reset Loophole

Here's something most screen time apps won't tell you: **anyone can bypass Screen Time by signing out of their Apple ID and factory resetting their device**.

It takes about 5 minutes. You lose your data, but you get a clean phone with no restrictions.

Latch closes this loophole. During setup, we walk you through:

1. **Blocking account changes** in Content & Privacy Restrictions, so you can't sign out of your Apple ID
2. **Setting a recovery Apple ID** that belongs to Latch, not you — so you can't reset the passcode through Apple's recovery flow
3. **Disabling passcode changes** so the settings can't be modified without the Screen Time passcode you don't have

Is it escapable? Everything is, if you try hard enough. But the goal isn't to make it impossible — it's to make it *inconvenient enough* that you don't bother.
